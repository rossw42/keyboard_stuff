## 2. Pre-Assembly Preparation

### Step 2.1: Inspect Components

**Time**: 5 minutes

1. **Verify all parts are present**:
   - Top frame (wood)
   - Bottom tray (wood)
   - 6× brass inserts
   - 6× M2 screws
   - 6× M3 screws
   - 4× rubber feet

2. **Inspect wood parts for damage**:
   - Check for cracks, chips, or dents
   - Verify finish is smooth and even
   - Ensure no dust or debris in cavities

3. **Verify hardware**:
   - Count all screws and inserts
   - Check brass inserts for damage or deformation
   - Verify screw threads are clean

**⚠️ STOP**: If any parts are damaged or missing, do not proceed. Contact supplier or manufacturer.

---

### Step 2.2: Clean Components

**Time**: 5 minutes

1. **Clean wood surfaces**:
   - Wipe all surfaces with dry lint-free cloth
   - Remove any dust from cavities
   - Do not use water or solvents on finished wood

2. **Clean brass inserts**:
   - Wipe with isopropyl alcohol if needed
   - Ensure threads are clean and free of debris

3. **Prepare work area**:
   - Clear flat, stable work surface
   - Lay down protective mat
   - Organize tools and hardware in parts tray
   - Ensure good lighting

## 3. Brass Insert Installation

**Time**: 15-20 minutes  
**Critical Step**: Take your time - this is permanent!

### Method A: Soldering Iron Installation (Recommended)

#### Step 3.1: Prepare Soldering Iron

1. Set soldering iron to 260-280°C (500-535°F)
2. If available, install brass insert installation tip
3. Allow iron to reach temperature (5 minutes)
4. Clean tip with brass sponge

**⚠️ WARNING**: 
- Temperature too high (>300°C) will scorch wood
- Temperature too low (<250°C) will not melt insert into wood properly

---

#### Step 3.2: Install Brass Inserts (Top Frame)

**Location Reference**: 6 standoff pockets in top frame cavity

```
Top Frame (viewed from above, cavity side up)

    Front Edge
    ┌─────────────────────────┐
    │  ●         ●         ●  │  ← Front row (3 inserts)
    │                         │
    │                         │
    │  ●         ●         ●  │  ← Rear row (3 inserts)
    └─────────────────────────┘
    Rear Edge
```

**For Each Insert (repeat 6 times)**:

1. **Position insert**:
   - Place brass insert on top of pilot hole in standoff pocket
   - Ensure insert is perpendicular to surface
   - Knurled side should face up (threads face down into hole)

2. **Apply heat**:
   - Place hot soldering iron tip on top of brass insert
   - Apply gentle downward pressure (approximately 2-3 lbs)
   - Insert will begin to sink into wood as brass heats and melts into wood fibers

3. **Monitor insertion**:
   - Insert should sink smoothly and evenly
   - Keep iron perpendicular to prevent tilting
   - Stop when insert is flush with pocket floor (should sit 2mm below top surface)
   - Typical insertion time: 10-15 seconds per insert

4. **Remove heat and allow to cool**:
   - Lift soldering iron straight up
   - Do not disturb insert for 30 seconds while it cools
   - Wood around insert may be slightly darkened (normal)

5. **Verify installation**:
   - Check insert is flush with pocket floor (±0.1mm)
   - Verify insert is perpendicular (not tilted)
   - Test threads with M3 screw (should thread smoothly by hand)

**✓ Quality Check After Each Insert**:
- [ ] Insert is flush with pocket floor
- [ ] Insert is perpendicular (not tilted)
- [ ] M3 screw threads smoothly for at least 8mm depth
- [ ] No excessive wood scorching around insert

**⚠️ TROUBLESHOOTING**:
- **Insert tilted**: If caught early, reheat and straighten. If cooled, may need to drill out and install new insert.
- **Insert too deep**: Cannot be fixed - may affect PCB clearance. Test fit PCB before proceeding.
- **Insert too shallow**: Reheat and press deeper.
- **Wood scorched**: Cosmetic only if not excessive. Reduce iron temperature for remaining inserts.

---

### Method B: Arbor Press Installation (Alternative)

**If you have access to an arbor press**:

1. **Prepare press**:
   - Install flat pressing surface or insert installation tool
   - Position top frame on press bed (cavity side up)
   - Support part evenly

2. **Install each insert**:
   - Place brass insert on pilot hole
   - Align press ram with insert
   - Apply slow, steady pressure
   - Insert should press in smoothly
   - Stop when flush with pocket floor

3. **Verify installation** (same as Method A, step 5)

**Note**: Press installation is faster but requires specialized equipment. Soldering iron method is more accessible for most users.

## 4. Finishing Application

**Time**: Variable (depends on finish type)  
**Note**: This step may have been completed during manufacturing. If parts are already finished, skip to Section 5.

### If Applying Finish Yourself:

Refer to finishing supplies in Bill of Materials. General process:

1. **Sand progressively**: 120 → 220 → 320 grit
2. **Remove dust**: Tack cloth between grits
3. **Apply finish**: Follow product instructions
   - Oil finish: 3-4 thin coats, 4-6 hours between coats
   - Wax finish: 2-3 coats, 2-4 hours between coats
   - Polyurethane: 3-4 coats, 2-4 hours between coats
4. **Light sand between coats**: 320 grit, very light
5. **Final cure**: 24-48 hours before assembly

**⚠️ IMPORTANT**: Ensure finish is fully cured before proceeding with assembly. Uncured finish can transfer to PCB or affect hardware installation.

## 5. PCB Mounting

**Time**: 10-15 minutes  
**Torque Specification**: 0.4 Nm (3.5 in-lbs) maximum

### Step 5.1: Prepare PCB

1. **Inspect PCB**:
   - Verify all switches are installed and seated properly
   - Check for any loose components
   - Ensure PCB is clean (no flux residue or debris)

2. **Test PCB** (recommended):
   - Connect PCB to computer via USB
   - Test all switches function correctly
   - Disconnect after testing

**💡 TIP**: Testing before assembly makes troubleshooting easier if issues arise later.

---

### Step 5.2: Position PCB in Top Frame

```
Side View - PCB Mounting

Top Frame:
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                     │ ← 5mm thick top frame
│  ┌───────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │ PCB (sits on standoffs)       │  │ ← PCB rests on standoff tops
│  └───────────────────────────────┘  │
│   ↑ ↑ ↑                     ↑ ↑ ↑   │ ← Standoffs with brass inserts
└───────────────────────────────────────┘
    3.8mm deep cavity
```

1. **Orient PCB correctly**:
   - USB port should face designated front edge
   - Verify switch orientation matches case design
   - Check PCB mounting holes align with standoffs

2. **Lower PCB into cavity**:
   - Hold PCB by edges (avoid touching components)
   - Carefully lower into top frame cavity
   - PCB should rest on standoff tops
   - Verify all 6 mounting holes align with brass inserts

3. **Check fit**:
   - PCB should sit flat (no rocking)
   - 0.5-1mm clearance around PCB perimeter
   - No interference with cavity walls
   - USB port accessible through case opening

**⚠️ STOP**: If PCB does not fit properly, do not force it. See Troubleshooting section.

---

### Step 5.3: Install M2 Mounting Screws

**Screw Locations**: 6 positions matching brass insert locations

```
PCB Mounting Screw Pattern (top view)

    Front Edge (USB port)
    ┌─────────────────────────┐
    │  1         2         3  │  ← Front row
    │                         │
    │         PCB             │
    │                         │
    │  4         5         6  │  ← Rear row
    └─────────────────────────┘
    Rear Edge
```

**Installation Sequence** (star pattern for even pressure):

1. **Start with screw #1** (front-left):
   - Insert M2 × 6mm screw through PCB hole
   - Thread into brass insert by hand (should turn easily)
   - Tighten finger-tight only (do not fully tighten yet)

2. **Install screw #6** (rear-right, opposite corner):
   - Same procedure as screw #1
   - Finger-tight only

3. **Install remaining screws in order**: #3, #4, #2, #5
   - Each screw finger-tight only

4. **Final tightening** (same star pattern: 1, 6, 3, 4, 2, 5):
   - Use torque screwdriver set to 0.4 Nm (3.5 in-lbs)
   - If no torque driver: tighten gently until snug, then 1/4 turn more
   - **DO NOT OVERTIGHTEN** - PCB can crack or brass inserts can pull out

**✓ Quality Check**:
- [ ] All 6 screws installed and tightened
- [ ] PCB sits flat and stable (no rocking)
- [ ] No cracks visible in PCB
- [ ] Screws are snug but not over-tightened
- [ ] USB port still accessible

**⚠️ WARNING**: 
- Over-tightening M2 screws is the #1 cause of PCB damage
- If you feel significant resistance, STOP and check alignment
- Brass inserts can pull out of wood if over-torqued

## 6. Case Assembly

**Time**: 10 minutes  
**Torque Specification**: 0.8-1.0 Nm (7-9 in-lbs)

### Step 6.1: Prepare Bottom Tray

1. **Inspect bottom tray**:
   - Verify cavity is clean and free of debris
   - Check countersunk holes are clean
   - Ensure finish is not damaged

2. **Prepare M3 screws**:
   - Lay out 6× M3 × 12mm flat head screws
   - Verify threads are clean
   - Have screwdriver ready

---

### Step 6.2: Mate Top Frame to Bottom Tray

```
Assembly View (exploded)

Top Frame with PCB mounted
        ↓
┌─────────────────────────┐
│   [PCB with switches]   │
│  ┌───────────────────┐  │
│  │                   │  │
│  └───────────────────┘  │
└─────────────────────────┘
        ↓ (lower into)
┌─────────────────────────┐
│  ┌───────────────────┐  │
│  │    (cavity)       │  │
│  └───────────────────┘  │
└─────────────────────────┘
Bottom Tray
```

1. **Position bottom tray**:
   - Place bottom tray on work surface (cavity side up)
   - Ensure surface is clean and flat

2. **Lower top frame assembly**:
   - Hold top frame with PCB mounted (PCB facing up)
   - Carefully lower top frame into bottom tray cavity
   - Align edges - parts should mate naturally
   - PCB and switches should nestle into bottom tray cavity

3. **Verify alignment**:
   - Check all edges are flush
   - Verify no gaps between top frame and bottom tray rim
   - Ensure mounting holes align (peek through from bottom)

**💡 TIP**: If alignment is difficult, use masking tape on one edge to act as a hinge while you position the parts.

---

### Step 6.3: Install M3 Case Screws

**Screw Locations**: 6 positions on bottom surface

```
Bottom View - Screw Pattern

    Front Edge
    ┌─────────────────────────┐
    │  ●         ●         ●  │  ← Front row (screws 1, 2, 3)
    │                         │
    │                         │
    │  ●         ●         ●  │  ← Rear row (screws 4, 5, 6)
    └─────────────────────────┘
    Rear Edge
```

**Installation Sequence** (star pattern):

1. **Flip assembly over** (bottom side up):
   - Hold parts together while flipping
   - Place on work surface
   - Bottom tray should be facing up

2. **Start with screw #1** (front-left):
   - Insert M3 × 12mm flat head screw through countersunk hole
   - Screw will pass through bottom tray floor and thread into brass insert in top frame
   - Thread by hand until screw head contacts countersink
   - Tighten finger-tight only (do not fully tighten yet)

3. **Install screw #6** (rear-right, opposite corner):
   - Same procedure as screw #1
   - Finger-tight only

4. **Install remaining screws in order**: #3, #4, #2, #5
   - Each screw finger-tight only
   - Verify all screws are started before final tightening

5. **Final tightening** (same star pattern: 1, 6, 3, 4, 2, 5):
   - Use torque screwdriver set to 0.8-1.0 Nm (7-9 in-lbs)
   - If no torque driver: tighten until snug, then 1/8 turn more
   - Screw heads should sit flush or slightly below bottom surface
   - Do not overtighten - wood can crack or brass inserts can pull out

**✓ Quality Check**:
- [ ] All 6 screws installed and tightened
- [ ] Screw heads flush with bottom surface
- [ ] No gaps between top frame and bottom tray
- [ ] Case feels solid (no flexing or creaking)
- [ ] No cracks visible in wood

---

### Step 6.4: Verify Assembly

1. **Visual inspection**:
   - Check all edges are flush
   - Verify no gaps between parts
   - Ensure finish is not damaged

2. **Functional test**:
   - Gently flex case (should feel solid)
   - Check for any creaking or movement
   - Verify USB port is accessible

3. **Flip case over** (right-side up):
   - Switches and keycaps should face up
   - Case should sit flat on work surface

## 7. Final Installation

**Time**: 5 minutes

### Step 7.1: Install Rubber Feet

**Foot Locations**: 4 corners of bottom surface

```
Bottom View - Rubber Foot Placement

    Front Edge
    ┌─────────────────────────┐
    │ ●                     ● │  ← Front corners
    │                         │
    │                         │
    │ ●                     ● │  ← Rear corners
    └─────────────────────────┘
    Rear Edge

Position: 10-15mm from each edge
```

1. **Clean bottom surface**:
   - Wipe bottom surface with isopropyl alcohol
   - Allow to dry completely (30 seconds)
   - Ensure surface is free of dust and oils

2. **Position rubber feet**:
   - Peel backing from first rubber foot
   - Position 10-15mm from corner (both edges)
   - Press firmly for 10 seconds
   - Repeat for remaining 3 feet

3. **Verify placement**:
   - All 4 feet should be equidistant from edges
   - Case should sit level on flat surface
   - Feet should provide 2mm clearance for USB cable

**💡 TIP**: Mark foot positions with pencil before removing adhesive backing to ensure even placement.

---

### Step 7.2: Install Keycaps

1. **Install keycaps on switches**:
   - Press keycaps onto switch stems
   - Ensure each keycap is fully seated
   - Verify correct keycap layout

2. **Visual check**:
   - All keycaps should be level
   - No keycaps rubbing against case
   - Keycaps should have consistent height

---

### Step 7.3: Final Testing

1. **Connect to computer**:
   - Plug USB cable into keyboard
   - Connect to computer
   - Wait for device recognition

2. **Test all keys**:
   - Use key testing software or text editor
   - Press each key to verify function
   - Test modifier keys (Shift, Ctrl, Alt, etc.)
   - Test function layer if applicable

3. **Physical inspection**:
   - Check case for any issues
   - Verify no rattling or loose parts
   - Ensure case sits flat and stable

**✓ Final Quality Check**:
- [ ] All keys function correctly
- [ ] Case is solid with no flexing
- [ ] No gaps or misalignment
- [ ] Rubber feet installed and level
- [ ] Finish is undamaged
- [ ] USB port accessible and functional

## 8. Troubleshooting

### Issue: PCB doesn't fit in cavity

**Possible Causes**:
- Cavity too small
- PCB dimensions incorrect
- Switches interfering with walls

**Solutions**:
1. Verify PCB is correct size (60% layout)
2. Check for debris in cavity
3. If cavity is slightly tight, can carefully sand walls with 220 grit sandpaper
4. Ensure switches are fully seated in PCB

---

### Issue: PCB mounting holes don't align with brass inserts

**Possible Causes**:
- Brass inserts installed in wrong locations
- PCB oriented incorrectly
- Manufacturing tolerance issue

**Solutions**:
1. Verify PCB orientation (USB port should face front)
2. Check brass insert locations against design
3. If slightly misaligned, can carefully enlarge PCB holes with 2.5mm drill bit
4. If severely misaligned, may need to drill out and reinstall brass inserts

---

### Issue: M2 screws won't thread into brass inserts

**Possible Causes**:
- Brass insert threads damaged
- Wrong screw size
- Insert not fully seated

**Solutions**:
1. Verify using M2 screws (not M3)
2. Try threading screw by hand without PCB to test insert
3. If threads damaged, may need to drill out and install new insert
4. Clean threads with compressed air

---

### Issue: Brass insert pulled out during screw tightening

**Possible Causes**:
- Insert not properly installed
- Over-tightening screws
- Wood too soft or damaged

**Solutions**:
1. Remove damaged insert
2. Fill hole with wood glue and sawdust mixture
3. Allow to cure 24 hours
4. Re-drill pilot hole and install new insert
5. Use lower torque when tightening screws

---

### Issue: Gaps between top frame and bottom tray

**Possible Causes**:
- Parts not fully seated
- Debris between parts
- Warped wood
- Screws not tightened evenly

**Solutions**:
1. Disassemble and clean mating surfaces
2. Check for debris or finish buildup
3. Reassemble and tighten screws in star pattern
4. If wood is warped, may need to sand high spots on mating surfaces

---

### Issue: Case flexes or creaks

**Possible Causes**:
- Screws not tight enough
- Floor thickness too thin
- Gaps between parts

**Solutions**:
1. Check all M3 screws are properly tightened (0.8-1.0 Nm)
2. Verify no gaps between top and bottom parts
3. If floor is too thin (<1.8mm), case may need reinforcement or replacement

---

### Issue: Keys not working after assembly

**Possible Causes**:
- PCB damaged during assembly
- USB connection issue
- Switch or PCB defect

**Solutions**:
1. Disconnect and reconnect USB cable
2. Try different USB port or cable
3. Disassemble and inspect PCB for cracks (especially around screw holes)
4. Test PCB outside of case to isolate issue
5. Check for shorts between PCB and case (ensure no metal contact)

---

### Issue: Rubber feet won't stick

**Possible Causes**:
- Surface not clean
- Oil or finish residue
- Low-quality adhesive

**Solutions**:
1. Clean surface thoroughly with isopropyl alcohol
2. Allow surface to dry completely
3. Lightly sand area with 320 grit (if finish allows)
4. Use stronger adhesive (CA glue) if needed
5. Consider mechanical attachment (small screws) as alternative

## Maintenance and Care

### Daily Use
- Wipe case with dry microfiber cloth to remove dust
- Avoid eating or drinking over keyboard
- Keep away from direct sunlight (can fade wood)

### Periodic Maintenance
- Every 3-6 months: Remove keycaps and clean switches
- Check screw tightness annually
- Reapply wood finish every 1-2 years if needed (oil finishes)

### Cleaning
- **Wood surfaces**: Dry cloth only, or slightly damp if needed
- **Do not use**: Harsh chemicals, alcohol wipes, or abrasive cleaners
- **Spills**: Wipe immediately, allow to dry completely

### Storage
- Store in cool, dry location
- Avoid extreme temperature or humidity changes
- If storing long-term, disconnect USB and store in protective case

---

## Safety Warnings

**⚠️ ELECTRICAL**:
- Disconnect power before disassembly
- Do not use if PCB shows signs of damage
- Keep away from liquids

**⚠️ MECHANICAL**:
- Do not overtighten screws - can damage PCB or wood
- Use proper tools to avoid stripping screws
- Support case when flipping to prevent dropping

**⚠️ HEAT**:
- Soldering iron is extremely hot (260-280°C)
- Allow brass inserts to cool before touching
- Use in well-ventilated area
- Keep away from flammable materials

**⚠️ WOOD**:
- Wood is natural material and may have variations
- Avoid impact or dropping case
- Do not expose to excessive moisture
